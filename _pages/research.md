## Research Philosophy

### Object, Properties, Theory

My research style is **object-oriented**: to find a meaningful object of study, continuously uncover **its properties**, and understand it more deeply. I believe that sufficiently deep understanding of an object may eventually lead to new theories.

For me, the object might be a neural representation, a latent structure in population activity, or a pattern that remains stable across tasks, individuals, or recording conditions. I am especially interested in objects that are both biologically meaningful and mathematically describable.

### Mathematical Lenses

Much of my thinking comes from mathematics.

- **Analysis** teaches me to ask about limits, convergence, and stability.
- **Algebra** teaches me to construct relationships between objects step by step.
- **Geometry** gives me intuition and urges me to look for invariants.
- **Probability theory and statistics** remind me that one can derive deep understanding from limited information.

I hope to use these tools not as decoration, but as ways to make scientific questions sharper.

### A Small Motto

> Science asks what is true.  
> Art tells what looks good.  
> Scientific Research combines the two.

I do not yet know exactly where these thoughts will lead. But I like questions that force me to learn both the biology and the mathematics more honestly.
