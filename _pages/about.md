# About Me [📄 Download my CV](/files/resume.pdf)

I am an undergraduate student in **Data Science** at **Fudan University**, interested in understanding how the brain works through physical principles, theoretical modeling, and data science. I am also interested in neural coding and brain–computer interfaces, especially in discovering more robust and generalizable patterns of neural representation. 

My academic background is interdisciplinary. Before switching my major to Data Science, I studied clinical medicine at **Shanghai Medical College of Fudan University** for two years (2024–2026). During that time, I also joined [Shu Lab at ITBR](https://youshenglab.fudan.edu.cn/) to study neurobiology. My passion for scientific research was sparked during my years at **Shenzhen Middle School** (2018–2024). I was surrounded by many bright and curious minds who thought independently, discussed ideas intensely, and created an atmosphere that I truly enjoyed.

As an undergrad, I'm still figuring things out, and I'd be thrilled to connect with anyone fascinated by how the brain works — and how to figure it out. Whether you're from neuroscience, AI, medicine, or just curious, feel free to reach out: [naonaozhong16@gmail.com](mailto:naonaozhong16@gmail.com)

# 🔬 About Research

- My research preference is more **object-oriented**: to find a meaningful object of study, continuously uncover its properties, and understand it more deeply. I believe that sufficiently deep understanding of an object will eventually lead to new theories.
- For now, I am more interested in approaching the problem from the perspective of **prediction**: as **larger and richer datasets** become available, perhaps we can observe new structures, constraints, and principles hidden within neural systems.  

Three questions fascinate me most:

- Latent space: physically instantiated substrate or informational mixture?
- Optimal level for neural computation: single neuron, connectome, or population activity?
- Mathematical structure of data: towards an information-theoretic characterization.

> Science asks what is true.  
> Art tells what looks good.
